Frequently Asked Questions

It depends on the setting, foot traffic, and the look you want. Gravel and stepping-stone paths suit informal or naturalized areas well; paver and natural stone walkways work well for main entries and higher-traffic routes.

Proper base preparation — excavation, compacted sub-base, and edging — is what keeps a walkway stable and prevents shifting or settling over time.

We take existing grades and water flow into account when siting and building walkways and stone features so they don’t create new runoff or standing-water problems.

Gravel paths benefit from periodic top-dressing and edge maintenance. Paver and stone walkways are largely low-maintenance once properly installed, aside from routine cleaning and joint upkeep.
